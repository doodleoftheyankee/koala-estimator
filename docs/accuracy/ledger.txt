One entry per value change: id, what changed, a before -> after example with real numbers, the

"Seeded" means the pricing every new database starts with (supabase/migrations), which equals the

=== Stage "money": the engine's money lines and option rules ===

L1  Each item is priced from its own cost, the same in every option; the rest of the price is a
    "Travel and setup" line.
    Each work item = its cost x (1 + overhead) / (1 - target margin), rounded down to the dollar.
    Travel and setup = the price the costs ask for minus the work items (drive, trips, the $5 rounding).
    Example (seeded): attic 1,200 sq ft at R-19, old insulation removed.
      Removal: Good $2,720.15 / Better $2,735.84 -> $2,483 in both.
      Good: $2,720.15 + $3,664.85 -> $2,483 + $3,345 + Travel and setup $557.
      Totals unchanged: Good $6,385, Better $8,010.
    Owner decision: OD2.
    Tests: lib/pricing/phase1.test.ts "the same removal costs the same in every option; travel and
    setup is its own line", "the same insulation and air sealing cost the same in Better and Best",
    property "the same work is the same price in every option, whatever the drive and the overrides".

L2  The minimum job top-up is its own "Minimum job charge" line.
    Example (seeded today): walls 1,000 sq ft, 2x4, Good: one item $1,500 -> $820 work + $190 travel
      and setup + $490 minimum job charge = $1,500.
    The record's example ($1,254 + $186 + $60) used the dense-pack wall bag (122.5 sq ft x in per
      bag, 31 bags) that the insulation stage adds (L20). With that coverage the engine gives exactly
      $1,254 + $186 + $60.
    After L20 (stage "cavity") the seeded example itself reads $1,254 + $186 + $60.
    Owner decision: OD2.
    Tests: phase1.test.ts "a job under the minimum shows its work, travel and the top-up to the
    minimum", "with the dense-pack wall coverage of 122.5 sq ft x in per bag, the record example
    reads $1,254 + $186 + $60".

L3  An estimator's price is a "Price adjustment" line; items keep their prices.
    Example (seeded): attic 900 sq ft at R-19, Better set to $3,000: items $2,373.44 + $626.56 ->
      $2,153 + $568 + travel and setup $374 + price adjustment -$95 (model price $3,095).
      A price raised by hand: attic 1,000, Good $3,100 -> adjustment +$105 over $2,995.
    Owner decision: OD3.
    Tests: phase1.test.ts "Better set to $3,000 keeps its items and shows the change", "a price
    raised by hand shows a positive adjustment".

L4  Printed whole-dollar items add up to the printed total.
    The record's example (garages 1,002 + 2,526 + crawlspace 368, Better, $14,994 under $14,995)
      came from a random quote whose other inputs are not in the record; at those sizes with plain
      inputs the Better total is $10,865, so it cannot be re-run.
    Example (seeded): garage 1,002 sq ft (difficult access) + crawlspace 368 sq ft, Best: printed
      $6,154 + $2,072 + $283 = $8,509 under an $8,510 total -> $5,482 + $1,846 + $252 + travel
      and setup $930 = $8,510.
    Owner decision: OD4.
    Tests: phase1.test.ts "printed items add up to the printed total (S1-F2)", property "every
    customer line is whole dollars, only the adjustment can be negative, and the lines add up".

L5  A price override rounds to whole dollars, and the price step is at least $1.
    Examples:
      Seeded attic 1,000 sq ft, Good set to $4,321.50: $4,321.50 -> $4,322 (adjustment +$1,327).
      Deviation note, hand fixture Better set to $7,224.50: "you are quoting $7,224.50" -> "$7,225".
      Price rounding set to 0 (seeded attic 1,000, Good): $2,990.92 -> $2,991.
      Price advice (win model) with rounding 0, $1,000 loaded cost: $1,333.34 / $1,335.48 ->
        $1,334 / $1,336, the same step as the engine.
    Owner decision: OD4.
    Tests: phase1.test.ts "a price change is whole dollars (S1-F1, OD4)", "the deviation note quotes
    the whole-dollar price", "the price step is at least $1: a rounding of 0 prices to the dollar";
    lib/learning/deviation.test.ts "quotes a price typed with cents in whole dollars, and says when
    there is no reason" (was "formats cents and a missing reason", $5,560.50 over $4,815 before the
    fixture change); lib/learning/winRate.test.ts "rounds up to whole dollars when rounding is off
    (as the engine does, L5), and includes a ceiling off the step grid".

L6  An override under $1 is ignored, with an override_too_low warning; Apply waits for $1 or more.
    Example (seeded): attic 900 sq ft, Good $0.40: printed $0 -> $2,735 (the model price) plus

    Owner decision: R7 (review finding), OD4.
    Test: phase1.test.ts "a price under $1 is not used; the estimator is told (R7)".

=== Stage "measure": measuring guards and job status ===

None of these changes a price formula. They stop a wrong measurement from reaching a price, and
stop a verified job from changing status. The before -> after numbers are what the estimator or
the office sees.

L25 A pitch typed in degrees is understood, and nothing steeper than 24/12 is taken.
    A pitch is rise per 12 ("6", "6/12", "6:12", "3/6") or degrees ("30°", "30 deg", "30 degrees").
    A pitch over 24/12 (63.4°) is refused. A bare number over 24 says what it would be and how to
    type degrees. A bare number from 12 to 24 is still read as rise per 12, with a note giving its
    angle and how to type degrees. The pitch field has a text keyboard and a "Use 30°" button.
    Examples (footprint 40 x 30 = 1,200 sq ft, roofline):
      "30 deg": refused ("isn't valid"), 0 sq ft -> 30° = 6.93/12, x1.155, 1,386 sq ft.
      "100": 100/12, x8.393, 10,072 sq ft -> refused: 'Pitch "100" would be 100/12, steeper than
        24/12. Type the rise per 12, e.g. 6/12, or degrees, e.g. 30°.'
      "30": 30/12, x2.693, 3,231 sq ft -> refused: 'Pitch "30" would be 30/12, steeper than 24/12.
        For degrees, type 30°.' with a "Use 30° (6.93/12)" button.
      "18": 18/12, 2,163 sq ft, no word -> the same, with 'Pitch 18 is read as 18/12, a very steep
        roof (56.3°). If you meant 18°, type 18°.'
      e2e (42 x 30 less a 6 x 10 chimney): "30 deg" -> "Use 1,395 sq ft".
    The record gave x1.157 for 30°. 1 / cos 30° is 1.1547, so it reads x1.155 here.
    Owner decision: none needed (audit RF-06, S2-F13; the 24/12 limit is the record's).
    Tests: lib/measure/units.test.ts "reads a pitch typed in degrees as degrees", "takes nothing
    steeper than 24/12", "says why a pitch is refused, with a hint", "offers the degrees reading of a
    bare number that reads as a steep roof"; lib/measure/measure.test.ts "a pitch typed in degrees is
    understood; nothing over 24/12 (L25, RF-06, S2-F13)", "a bare number that reads as a very steep
    roof says how it was read (L25)"; e2e pitch step.
